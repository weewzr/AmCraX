# SIP Energy Carriers cost chart: source search (24 November 2026)

## Result in one paragraph
The chart "Cost Comparison of Energy Carriers" (USD/kg H2; LH2, MCH, NH3 with dehydrogenation, NH3 direct combustion, NH3 from CH4 + CCS with direct combustion) was found as printed slide 11 of a conference presentation by the SIP Energy Carriers programme director (Muraki, 22 August 2019, Melbourne; key `muraki2019ammoniakey`). The original Cabinet Office, JST or Institute of Applied Energy document with a table was NOT found. The slide gives no target year, currency year, distance, scale, feedstock-hydrogen cost, or definition of "dehydrogenation & refinement". Bar totals could not be read from it in this session, so every per-bar number in the project brief remains unverified.

## What was verified (verbatim text of the slide deck)
- Slide 11: title "Cost Comparison of Energy Carriers"; axis ticks $1/kg to $4/kg; text annotations "Ammonia $500/ton" and "$335/ton".
- Slide 10: "Estimated Import Price $330 ~ 340/ton ($2/kg-H2)" for CO2-free (blue) ammonia delivered to Japan for coal co-firing.
- Slide 2: hydrogen density NH3 121, LH2 71, MCH 23 kg H2 per m3.
- Slide 4: SIP 2014 to 2018, budget 16.7 billion yen (150 M USD).
- Slide 5: 50 kW and 300 kW turbines on 100 % ammonia, NOx under 10 ppm with an ordinary SCR; 2 MW turbine on 20 % ammonia co-fired.

Derived arithmetic (project calculation, not source text): 1 t NH3 holds 177.6 kg H2, so 500 USD/t is 2.82 and 335 USD/t is 1.89 USD/kg H2 (the slide itself says about 2 for 330 to 340). This supports the order of magnitude of the two NH3 direct-combustion bars (about 2.8 and about 1.9 to 2.1) but not the LH2, MCH or dehydrogenation values.

## What was not found
- Any numeric table, original yen/Nm3 values, exchange rate, target year (2030 is plausible but nothing read states it), transport distance, plant scale, feedstock-H2 cost.
- Scope of "dehydrogenation & refinement" (cracking plus purification, and to which purity).
- Any SIP-stated cost per kg H2 of cracking plus purification.
- Any SIP-stated efficiency or cost comparison of direct ammonia combustion against cracked ammonia or hydrogen.
- The official JST brochures (2015, 2016) resolve and were text-searched: no cost values (images not inspectable). The team 4 report PDF has no text layer.
- Not verified at all: Kobayashi et al. 2019, Proc. Combust. Inst. 37, 109-133 (doi 10.1016/j.proci.2018.09.029), SIP team review. Its bibliographic data came from search results only; no cost comparison confirmed in it. The METI fuel-ammonia supply-chain task force material (meti.go.jp, 2022) was seen only as a search hit. Several publisher pages returned HTTP 403 to automated requests (RSC); not retried.

## Assessment (comparison with published literature, not a measurement)
The slide is a published cost estimate by the programme leadership, presented without assumptions. It can motivate, not prove, the following.

(a) Direct ammonia use cheaper per unit of hydrogen energy than cracked ammonia. Supported in direction only: the slide's own NH3 bars (direct combustion against dehydrogenation) differ, and the two prices given (500 and 335 USD/t) give an ammonia-only cost near 2.8 or 1.9 USD/kg H2. Size of the gap is not citable until the bars are read from the graphic or the original table is found. Boundary: import chain to Japan, not a plant gate; costs per kg of hydrogen contained, not per kWh of electricity, so turbine efficiency differences between ammonia and hydrogen are not in it.

(b) Cracked ammonia comparable with LH2 and MCH when hydrogen is the product. The slide structure (three "CO2-free H2 to carrier to H2" bars) is exactly this comparison, and the unverified readings place all three within about 0.35 USD/kg. No verbatim values, so the claim "comparable" is not yet citable with numbers; it also depends on the unstated purity and unstated scale.

(c) Size of cracking plus purification cost. Not supported by any number read. The unverified reading (about 0.8 to 0.9 USD/kg) cannot be compared with AmCraX's 0.975 USD/kg H2 (reconversion, excluding ammonia feed, 100 kmol/h) until the original defines purity (fuel-cell grade or not), scale (import terminal versus 100 kmol/h NH3, which is 150 kmol/h H2 or about 7.3 t H2/day), and cost year.

(d) The 40 % in the essay (direct ammonia "about 40 % cheaper" than cracking-based use). Not supported by this source. Correction of 24 November 2026 (full text of Halim et al. 2025 read, `research/notes/halim2025_full_text.md`): the quoted figure is **not** a power generation cost. In Halim et al. it is 1 - LCOA/LCOH (39 % green, 44 % blue, p. 9), the cost of fuel delivered to the turbine per kg of hydrogen-equivalent fuel energy, with the power plant excluded; the paper computes no cost of electricity. It is also not this chart's quantity (hydrogen cost per kg along a carrier chain). Earlier wording, withdrawn: "The quoted figure is power generation cost (Halim et al.), not hydrogen cost per kg". At the unverified readings the saving from not dehydrogenating is about 0.8 to 0.9 of about 3.65, roughly 22 to 25 % per kg H2, a different quantity and a different boundary. Do not cite the SIP chart for 40 %.

Boundary differences limiting any comparison: SIP chart is a long-distance import chain (loading, ship, unloading, distribution) with feedstock hydrogen, an estimate for an unstated future year at large scale; AmCraX is a plant-gate model at 100 kmol/h, 2026 prices, with ammonia feed excluded in the 0.975 figure.

## Recommendation
Cite only the verbatim slide-10 and slide-11 text with the stated limits, or wait until the original SIP or Institute of Applied Energy table is located (candidate routes not tried: cao.go.jp SIP final report, JST SIP Energy Carriers final report PDFs, METI fuel ammonia roadmap documents). Do not cite the lecture readings.
