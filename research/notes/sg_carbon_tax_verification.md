# Singapore carbon tax boundary: verification against official sources

Access date for every item: 23 November 2026. Machine-readable version: `research/data/carbon_policy_sg.json`.
Quote classes: **statute** = copied from Singapore Statutes Online (exact); **page** = verbatim fragment returned by the fetch tool from an official government page (re-check before quoting in the report); **secondary** = law firm or press (context only).

## Result in one paragraph

The tax is charged on reckonable greenhouse gas emissions emitted directly from a single-site business facility that reaches 25,000 tCO2e per year (Scope 1 only). The Act fixes SGD 5 per tCO2e up to emissions year 2023, SGD 25 for 2024 and 2025 and SGD 45 for 2026 and every later year. The SGD 50 to 80 figure for 2030 is a stated government aim and is not in the Act. N2O is a covered gas (GWP 265 in the Act). Nothing in the definitions reaches emissions embodied in imported ammonia or other upstream emissions abroad. International credits may offset up to 5 per cent of taxable emissions (official pages; the statutory reference for the 5 per cent was not found). Transition allowances for existing emissions-intensive trade-exposed facilities cover only part of their emissions and exclude new investments.

## Unconfirmed or not read (state plainly in the report)

1. Section 16 (charging), ss 20A to 20G, 26 and 33B of the Act, and all subsidiary legislation. The Statutes Online site returned HTTP 202 with an empty body when requests came too close together; repeated attempts failed. The facts above come from the sections that were read (ss 1 to 7 via the whole-document view, 20, 33A, and the three Schedules).
2. The numerical 5 per cent credit limit has no statutory reference in what was read.
3. No official source says in words that imported-fuel or upstream emissions are outside the tax. The conclusion rests on the direct-emission definition (row 10).
4. The Ministry of Finance Budget 2022 statement and the Budget 2026 remark that the 2030 rate could sit at the lower end of the SGD 50 to 80 range: not retrieved from mof.gov.sg. The second claim appears only in a search-result summary of press coverage.
5. Which facilities hold EITE allowances, and any quantum: not published in the pages read.

## Points that matter for the modellers

- Use SGD 45/tCO2e for 2026 onward as the default. A higher value is a scenario, not law.
- N2O: the Act uses GWP 265 (IPCC AR5 basis, stated in the First Schedule), not the AR6 value of 273 used in the AmCraX LCA module. For the Singapore tax calculation use 265. For the hypothetical life-cycle scenario the choice is free but must be stated.
- A new ammonia cracker furnace fired with fuel gas pays tax on its CO2 if the facility reaches 25,000 tCO2e per year (aggregated over the single site, not per unit). Heat from ammonia or hydrogen gives no CO2.
- A facility is taxed on the site total, so a small plant on a large site is judged on the site's total reckonable emissions (s 3 defines the site and s 3(3) joins contiguous or dependent parcels under one operational control).
- Policy statements say allowances exclude new investments, so a hypothetical new plant should not be given allowances by default.
