## 3. What their work can and cannot contribute

- **Do they work on H2/N2 separation at cracker conditions? No.** Nothing found, from either researcher, covers H2/N2 separation, Pd or Pd-alloy membranes, high-temperature (above 300 degC) membranes, ammonia, catalytic membrane reactors or hydrogen purification from cracked ammonia. Their gas-separation work is on polymeric, mixed-matrix, MOF, COF and graphene-type membranes, almost all at ambient to moderate temperature and mostly for CO2 capture and H2/CO2.
- **Quantitatively usable in AmCraX:** nothing. No number from their papers was read, and none of the studied conditions (temperature, gas pair, NH3) matches the cracker effluent.
- **Context only:** (a) the polymer and MOF-membrane route is a possible low-temperature alternative to Pd for H2 purification (after cooling, and after NH3 removal), but its H2/N2 selectivity is typically orders of magnitude below Pd-Ag (400,000 in Cechetto 2021, ideal, 450 degC); that comparison is from other sources, not from their papers; (b) hollow-fibre module modelling (item 2) as a pointer for module design.
- **What would be needed to use them:** the full texts of items 1 and 4 to read H2 permeance and H2/N2 selectivity (if reported), with temperature, pressure and feed (dry or humid), and a statement of whether NH3 was tested. A direct enquiry about any unpublished H2/N2 data is the only route to quantitative use.
- **Caution for the essay or report:** do not write that NUS work supports membrane purification of cracked ammonia. The accurate wording is that NUS has strong membrane-science groups whose published gas-separation work is on non-metallic membranes at low temperature and does not address cracked-ammonia purification.
