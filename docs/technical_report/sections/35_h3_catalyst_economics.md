# 35 Catalysts (H3): prices, economics, kinetic validity and exchanger sizing

Hypothesis H3 states that low-temperature catalysts combined with heat integration reduce energy use and can outweigh their higher cost. Section 26 tested it on the Phase 1 basis with the published TEA catalyst prices. Phase 2 replaced those prices, re-ran the comparison at equal conversion, classified the validity of every rate law at the 20 bar operating pressure, and sized and costed the feed/effluent heat exchanger (FEHE). The results move against the essay on catalyst cost at the modelled Ni temperature, confirm the energy saving of heat integration, and leave its net cost saving dependent on an unsourced heat-transfer coefficient.

## 35.1 Catalyst prices

No supplier quotation (grade A) or published commercial price (grade B) exists for any ammonia-cracking catalyst; the product pages of the large vendors give no prices. Every ruthenium catalyst price in the TEA literature lies below the value of the ruthenium it contains. For example, Cho et al. (2026) use 542 USD/kg for Ru-K/CaO with 3 wt% Ru, while the metal content alone is worth 666 USD/kg at the USGS 2025 average ruthenium price (U.S. Geological Survey, 2026) and 1,784 USD/kg at the Johnson Matthey base price of 20 November 2026 (1,850 USD/oz, read from a secondary display; wallstreet-online.de, 2026). Phase 2 therefore derives prices from metal content plus a non-metal part: price = (w~Ni~/100) P~Ni~ + (w~Ru~/100) P~Ru~ + N, with N = 32.2 USD/kg (Cho's calculated 33.4 USD/kg for 8 wt% Ni/Al~2~O~3~ less its nickel). These are derived values (metal part grade E, non-metal part grade C), not quotations. The published TEA prices are kept only as a labelled historical case.

## 35.3 Energy at equal conversion (H3-a)

The Phase 1 Aspen comparison (Section 26) found a 17 % lower fired duty for Ru/Al~2~O~3~ at 499 °C than for Ni/MgO at 594 °C, with 100 kg of catalyst each. Those runs reach unequal conversion (Ni 0.9589, Ru 0.9496), so less unconverted NH~3~ reaches the tail gas with Ni and Ni needs more fired heat. At equal conversion of 0.95 and 20 bar (independent Python calculation, cross-checked against Aspen Plus V15 at the same temperature and conversion), the fired duty is 485.1 kW for Ru/Al~2~O~3~ against 510.3 kW for Ni/MgO with heat integration, a saving of 25.1 kW, and 163 kW without heat integration. The size depends on the metric: 25.1 kW is 4.9 % of the supplementary fired duty but only 1.1 % of the 2293 kW total external heating. The equal-conversion result is Python only, because the Aspen runs reach unequal conversion. When the designs are optimised for cost instead, the Ru designs run hotter and use 19 to 21 kW more energy than the Ni design.

The model resolution can be read in two ways, and both are stated. On the absolute Python-Aspen offset in fired duty ({{VAL:catalyst_economics/catalyst_economics.json#resolution/max_abs_fired_duty_offset_kW|.1f}} kW; {{VAL:catalyst_economics/catalyst_economics.json#resolution/max_abs_python_minus_aspen_lcoh_USD_per_kg|.4f}} USD/kg in LCOH), which is the reading used for the verdicts, the saving is resolved only for the two Ru catalysts, at temperature differences of 72 K or more (Ru/MgO 18.6 kW at 522 °C and Ru/Al~2~O~3~ 25.1 kW at 499 °C against 594 °C); for the NiRu catalysts it is 4 to 5 kW and not resolved. Ru/MgO is resolved by only 3.4 kW, and its temperature rests on an extrapolated law. On the spread of that offset across the catalysts ({{VAL:catalyst_economics/catalyst_economics.json#resolution/spread_of_fired_duty_offset_between_catalysts_kW|.1f}} kW; {{VAL:catalyst_economics/catalyst_economics.json#resolution/spread_of_offset_between_catalysts_USD_per_kg|.4f}} USD/kg), which applies when the offset is common to all designs, the NiRu savings of 4 to 5 kW would also be resolved. The Phase 1 figure of 17 % is withdrawn. Heat integration also saves less at low temperature: the net heat-integration saving is 0.099 USD/kg for Ru/Al~2~O~3~ at 499 °C against 0.132 USD/kg for Ni at 594 °C.

## 35.4 Catalyst economics (H3-c)

**Against the essay.** With heat integration, at equal conversion of 0.95, derived central prices, the FEHE costed and the Ni reference at its AmCraX-modelled 594 °C (an extrapolated law), three of the four low-temperature catalysts are dearer than Ni/MgO beyond the model resolution of 0.006 USD/kg: Ru/Al~2~O~3~ +0.081, Ru/MgO +0.043 and 5Ni1Ru +0.011 USD/kg H~2~ (+0.105, +0.056, +0.015 SGD/kg). 2.5Ni0.5Ru (+0.005 USD/kg) is within resolution. Without heat integration the two NiRu catalysts are slightly cheaper (5Ni1Ru -0.0010, 2.5Ni0.5Ru -0.0056 USD/kg). Against the sourced Ni design temperatures (736 and 760 °C) every low-temperature design is cheaper (see below). The break-even catalyst prices, with each design fixed at its equal-conversion temperature (exchanger mode, central inputs), are 1506, 1297, 177 and 151 USD/kg, well below the derived central prices (4791, 3006, 628, 330); against the metal floors (1775, 1109, 223, 111) they are lower for Ru/Al~2~O~3~ and 5Ni1Ru and higher for Ru/MgO and 2.5Ni0.5Ru. Only on one basis, against the re-optimised Ni design at the central price, do the break-even prices (630, 313, 92 and 98 USD/kg) all fall below the metal floors and hence below the Ru metal value. When temperature and catalyst mass are re-optimised for cost, the optimum for an expensive catalyst is a small charge at high temperature: Ru/Al~2~O~3~ 614 °C with 7.5 kg, Ru/MgO 622 °C with 14.3 kg, against Ni/MgO at 537 °C with 539 kg. The low-temperature operation the hypothesis needs is then not chosen, and no low-temperature catalyst is cheaper by more than the resolution of 0.0060 USD/kg. In the sensitivity set a resolved low-temperature win occurs in 1 of 100 rows (2.5Ni0.5Ru with the Ni rate divided by 5). The NPV of choosing the low-temperature catalyst is -1.78, -0.99, -0.25 and -0.10 MUSD, with no payback.

The verdict is nevertheless INCONCLUSIVE, not CONTRADICTED, because the outcome depends on the nickel reference temperature. Against Ni at the sourced design temperatures of 735.52 °C (Cho et al., 2026) and 760 °C (Mehr et al., 2026), the low-temperature designs win; the Ni temperatures at which each catalyst breaks even, which do not need a Ni rate law, are 670 °C (Ru/Al~2~O~3~), 676 °C (Ru/MgO), 640 °C (5Ni1Ru) and 540 °C (2.5Ni0.5Ru). The AmCraX Ni/MgO law, which is in class "extrapolated", puts the Ni optimum at 537 to 549 °C. The sourced values and the model straddle the thresholds. With rate laws validated against literature only, the kinetics-independent energy result, the price and break-even arithmetic and these Ni threshold temperatures survive; every ranking against Ni/MgO does not.

## 35.5 Heat-exchanger sizing and the net value of heat integration (H3-d)

The baseline FEHE transfers 612.2 kW, from a hot side cooling from 600 to 228.4 °C to a cold side heating from 75.9 to 550 °C. The temperatures cross, so one 1-2 shell is infeasible; the design is five 1-2 shells in series with F = 0.84, an LMTD of 91.9 K and a segmented UA of 7.32 kW/K. No overall heat-transfer coefficient U at 20 bar was found in the literature. The VDI Heat Atlas (VDI, 2010; read from a search excerpt) gives 5 to 35 W/(m^2^ K) for gas on both sides at about 1 bar and 150 to 500 at 200 to 300 bar. The cases are therefore 35 (low), 150 (high) and their geometric mean of 72.5 W/(m^2^ K), a derived number, giving areas of 249, 120 and 58 m^2^. With the Turton et al. (2018) fixed-tube-sheet correlation (stainless steel, 19.5 barg, CEPCI 797.9) and the reference indirect factor of 1.4336, the total-capital equivalent is 1.784, 1.542 and 1.437 MUSD (2.298, 1.987 and 1.852 MSGD); the Towler and Sinnott (2013) U-tube correlation gives 1.18 to 1.38 MUSD.

Heat integration saves 0.2138 USD/kg H~2~ (4.2292 against 4.0155), almost all supplementary NH~3~ fuel. After the annualised exchanger cost the net benefit is +0.067, +0.087 and +0.095 USD/kg at the low, central and high U, and +0.071 to +0.137 USD/kg in all ten catalyst cases. It turns negative for U below the break-even of 7.7 to 11.3 W/(m^2^ K) over the cases and correlations (11.3 for the baseline with Turton, 7.8 with Towler), and in four adverse corners: U = 10, NH~3~ at 300 USD/t (inside the sourced grey range) with low U, a capital recovery factor of 0.199 with low U, and a 20 K approach with low U; the worst case is -0.012 USD/kg. The break-even capital recovery factor, solved directly, is {{VAL:heat_exchanger/heat_exchanger_study.json#row_evidence/H3-d/breakeven_crf_solved/low|.3f}} / {{VAL:heat_exchanger/heat_exchanger_study.json#row_evidence/H3-d/breakeven_crf_solved/central|.3f}} / {{VAL:heat_exchanger/heat_exchanger_study.json#row_evidence/H3-d/breakeven_crf_solved/high|.3f}} at low, central and high U, against the reference 0.1102. The energy result is an Aspen Plus V15 result: the first-law heat deficit falls from 1385.1 to 772.9 kW (-44 %) and the total external heating from 2905.3 to 2293.1 kW (-21 %). The row's sub-claim says that heat integration reduces energy use "(and cost)", and a row with two halves needs both, as for H1-a. The energy half is SUPPORTED; the cost half depends on a U with no source at 20 bar, and the only sourced range (5 to 35 W/(m^2^ K) at about 1 bar) straddles the break-even. Row H3-d is therefore INCONCLUSIVE, with the energy reduction as its finding, and component H3-H is INCONCLUSIVE. Before the independent review this row was SUPPORTED with the cost half excluded from the vote (Section 31.4). The supplementary fuel is counted at 100 % of its lower heating value; at 0.85 the gross saving rises to {{VAL:reference_scenario/baseline.json#fuel_efficiency_sensitivity/cases/2/heat_integration_gross_saving_USD_per_kg|.3f}} USD/kg (Section 32.3). The exchanger cost is added on top of the reference CAPEX item, which may count part of it twice; this makes the net benefit conservative.

## 35.6 Rows H3-b, H3-e and H3-f

Row H3-b (the temperature ordering of the catalysts) is INCONCLUSIVE because it rests on rate laws extrapolated to 20 bar. Row H3-e tests the statement of Cho et al. (2026) that a Ru cracker needs no supplementary fuel while a Ni cracker does: that pattern exists only for PSA recoveries between 0.7564 and 0.7813, and Cho's own 0.756 lies 0.0004 below the Ni edge; it is INCONCLUSIVE. Row H3-f tests Cho's 7.0 % lower reconversion cost for Ru: AmCraX gives -4.5 to +1.6 % at Cho's prices and -16.2 to +1.0 % at the derived Ru-K/CaO price, depending on the inventory basis and PSA recovery; it is INCONCLUSIVE.
