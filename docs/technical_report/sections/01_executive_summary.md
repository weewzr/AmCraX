# 1 Executive summary

**What was built.** AmCraX (Ammonia Cracking Simulation and Optimisation Platform) models a plant that cracks refrigerated liquid ammonia into hydrogen. It has four layers: an Aspen Plus V15 flowsheet that is generated as an input file from a typed case definition and solved through the COM interface; independent Python models of the same plant (thermochemistry and equilibrium, a flowsheet mirror, pinch analysis, catalyst kinetics in a plug-flow reactor, separation, economics and carbon intensity); a FastAPI back end that runs Aspen in an isolated worker process; and a React dashboard. Every reported value carries its origin: **Aspen Plus V15 simulation**, **independent Python calculation**, **published literature** or **engineering assumption**.

**Evidence base.** The results rest on 279 Aspen runs, all converged: the baseline and its variant without heat integration, 8 named scenarios, 10 re-solved optimisation candidates, 91 sensitivity runs in 8 studies and a 168-point RGibbs equilibrium grid with PENG-ROB and IDEAL. Sixteen screenshots of the real Aspen Plus V15 user interface document the model (Sections 5 to 11). The software has 391 passing Python tests (one opt-in live-Aspen test is skipped by default) and 96 frontend unit tests.

**Process findings.** The cracker duty of 1480.0 kW is the largest heat demand. Burning the PSA tail gas covers about two-thirds of the external heating. The remaining deficit is a temperature-level problem as well as a quantity problem: reaction heat at 600 °C can only come from flue gas above 620 °C, which holds 977.5 kW against the 1480.0 kW the reaction needs (Section 18). Above about 550 °C the reactor temperature and pressure change efficiency and cost only slightly, because unconverted NH~3~ is burned for heat; the assumed PSA recovery and the exchanger are the strongest process levers (Section 17). The baseline product does not meet the ISO 14687:2019 grade D limits of 99.97 mol% H~2~ and 300 ppm N~2~ for fuel-cell road vehicles (International Organization for Standardization [ISO], 2019), so it is not fuel-cell grade; the purity-driven scenario reaches 99.979 mol% at a 1.2 % higher LCOH, again as a consequence of assumed split fractions. NH~3~ polishing to 0.1 ppm is required and is not modelled.

**Validation status.** Of the 137 validation items, 22 fail and are reported as failures. Twenty are the flowsheet cross-checks `FS-<case>`, which compare all key KPIs of Aspen and the Python model within 2 %. They fail because the two models use different property models in the feed section: Aspen PENG-ROB against the reference equation of state for ammonia with an ideal-liquid dew point. At identical states Aspen's vaporiser duty is 2.4 % above the reference equation (739.5 against 721.8 kW), and no reference NH~3~-H~2~O mixture model was available to decide the dew point. These items measure a model-form difference in both directions; they do not show that either model is the weaker one. The reaction-section comparison FS-PRIMARY (conversion, cracked-gas composition, reactor duty) passes for all 20 cases. The other two failures are kinetic: published conversion curves are reproduced only within about 0.11 against a 0.05 tolerance. Independent evidence comes from like-for-like equilibrium comparisons (Aspen IDEAL against Python ideal gas within 0.045 percentage points, PENG-ROB against Python Peng-Robinson within 0.185 percentage points, the latter gap unexplained), thermochemistry against NIST-JANAF, and recalculation of the flame temperature, flue-gas duty, pump power and exchanger UA outside Aspen (Sections 13, 15 and 16). Balance closures of about 10^-9^ show consistent extraction and bookkeeping, not correct physics. Validation also exposed one automation defect, a stale heat duty read during an asynchronous run, which was fixed and is now guarded (Section 12.4).

**Economics and optimisation.** Phase 2 put every research output on one unified reference scenario (Section 32): the baseline LCOH is 4.015 USD/kg H~2~ (5.174 SGD/kg at 1.288444 SGD/USD), with a CAPEX of 7.67 MUSD taken from the itemised costs of Cho et al. (2026), at the conservative end of the published range. The ammonia feed is 76 % of it and the NH~3~ price explains about 90 % of its variance; the break-even NH~3~ price against the 3.06 USD/kg NE Asia SMR marker is 320 USD/t, so grey ammonia at 450 USD/t is not cheap enough (Sections 34 and 37). The core sections keep the legacy correlation: its LCOH of 3.919 USD/kg H~2~ is a legacy core result, superseded for research outputs, and a preliminary order-of-magnitude estimate. The NH~3~ feed contributes 3.040 USD/kg (78 %), CAPEX 0.356, imported heat 0.268 and fixed operating cost 0.252 USD/kg. The CAPEX comes from a plant-level correlation that does not see exchanger area, PSA size or catalyst volume, cost years are mixed, and NO~x~ treatment, NH~3~ polishing, compression and installed-cost factors are omitted; this is why the result lies below the 5.1-6.4 USD/kg published at comparable ammonia prices (Section 19). On the Python process model the minimum LCOH, 3.805 USD/kg, lies at about 529 °C, 7.5 bar, recovery 0.95 and a 20 K exchanger approach; the recovery and approach sit on their bounds because the cost model does not see equipment size, and the objectives largely do not conflict. All ten optimisation candidates were re-solved in Aspen Plus V15: all converged, the primary metrics agree within 1.15 % and the LCOH within 0.23 % (Section 20).

**Public website and simulator.** A static build of the front end is prepared for GitHub Pages and is built to be served at https://weewzr.github.io/AmCraX/. Its first page is an interactive process simulator (Section 39): the rate laws, plug-flow reactor, cracker heat balance, economics on the unified basis and carbon accounting run in the browser, agree with the Python model to within 4e-8 in conversion and 2e-16 in LCOH, and every displayed value is labelled as a stored Aspen result, an interpolation or a browser-model calculation. The site never runs Aspen Plus and never contacts an API (Section 23). Live Aspen runs need the local platform (Section 22).

**Research expansion: four hypotheses for Singapore.** Sections 24 to 30 and 33 to 40 test four statements of a preceding literature essay for a representative Singapore industrial setting (Jurong Island; no facility is implied). Five published catalyst rate laws were implemented in Aspen Plus V15 RPlug reactors, three exactly and two as fitted approximations, and verified against the Python reactor; 162 further Aspen runs, all converged, cover the kinetic studies and ten full flowsheets with kinetic crackers (Section 25). These feed a catalyst and heat-integration comparison (Section 26), a Singapore cost basis with sensitivity and Monte Carlo analysis (Section 27), purification against every modelled ISO 14687:2019 limit (Section 28), end-use pathways (Section 29) and a life-cycle comparison of ammonia routes (Section 30). The verdicts, read from the evidence matrix at build time (Section 40), are:

* **H1** cracking is most justified when molecular hydrogen is needed: {{VERDICT:H1}};
* **H2** affordable, low-carbon ammonia is essential: {{VERDICT:H2}};
* **H3** low-temperature catalysts with heat integration reduce energy use and can outweigh their higher cost: {{VERDICT:H3}};
* **H4** purification must balance purity against recovery, energy and cost: {{VERDICT:H4}}.

The verdicts follow verdict methodology v2, fixed before the Phase 2 analysis (Section 40); under the rule of the 23 November 2026 release all four were INCONCLUSIVE, and both are shown in Section 40. Section 31 lists what changed since that release. The main Phase 2 findings are:

* **H1.** Under the modelled operating conditions, direct ammonia combustion produces electricity at a lower cost than the standalone cracking pathways, with a calculated cost advantage of approximately 8 to 17 % (5 to 15 % on the earlier cost basis). This comparison assumes that the hydrogen-rich turbine operates at the same 49.64 % efficiency as the direct-ammonia turbine. The actual economic advantage depends on fuel-specific turbine performance. Cracking with PSA would need a turbine 12.55 percentage points more efficient (9.68 to 18.10 over 11 bases), above the only sourced upper bound of 7.3; fired cracking would need 5.16 points, for which no source exists. Against the essay, exhaust-heated cracking is about 1.3 % cheaper than direct use on the reference basis (Section 33).
* **H2.** Feed is 76 % of the LCOH, and only ammonia at or below about 320 USD/t keeps cracked hydrogen competitive with the benchmark. Under the actual Singapore carbon tax the tax is zero for every ammonia route, so low-carbon ammonia earns no cost advantage; under a hypothetical life-cycle carbon price (not Singapore law) blue overtakes grey at about 33 SGD/t (range 12 to 605) and green at current prices needs about 305 SGD/t (Section 34).
* **H3.** At equal conversion a low-temperature catalyst saves about 5 % of fired duty with heat integration, not the 17 % of Phase 1, which came from unequal conversion. Against the essay, at ruthenium prices derived from metal content the low-temperature catalysts do not pay at equal conversion. Heat integration still pays after the exchanger cost: +0.067 to +0.095 USD/kg net (Section 35).
* **H4.** PSA against a Pd-Ag membrane is not robust either way: it turns on the PSA recovery, with parity near 0.80; the membrane is at TRL 4 to 5 against 9 for PSA (Section 36).

No hypothesis is supported as a whole, and AmCraX has no independent experimental validation; the evidence is sorted into numerical verification, Python against Aspen comparison and comparison with published literature (Section 38).

**Main limitations.** AmCraX is not a validated digital twin: it is steady state and uses no plant data, and the baseline cracker is an equilibrium reactor. The PSA is a reduced-order splitter whose 0.85 recovery is an assumption above the 0.71-0.81 literature range, the kinetics are fitted at 1 atm (one law at up to 10 bar) and extrapolated to 20 bar, the costs are preliminary and do not respond to equipment size, and several essay values could not be checked against their primary source (Section 41).
