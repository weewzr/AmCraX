# 42 Conclusions

## 42.1 Conclusions on the AmCraX model

1. **A reproducible Aspen model.** The Aspen Plus V15 flowsheet is generated from a typed case object and solved without manual GUI work. All 279 core Aspen runs converged, every stored case names the input file that reproduces it, and the COM-driven temperature sweep agrees with Aspen's own Sensitivity block to 4.8 × 10^-10^ (Sections 6, 12 and 17).
2. **The baseline is heat-limited, not conversion-limited.** At 600 °C and 20 bar the equilibrium conversion is 0.9808 and the plant delivers 251.6 kg/h of H~2~. The cracker duty of 1480.0 kW dominates the 2293.1 kW of external heating. The feed/effluent exchanger recovers 612.2 kW and lowers the LCOH by 0.214 USD/kg before its own cost; after the exchanger cost the net benefit is +0.067 to +0.095 USD/kg on the reference basis, a result that rests on an overall heat-transfer coefficient with no source at 20 bar (Sections 5, 10 and 35).
3. **Tail gas alone cannot heat the cracker.** On a first-law basis tail-gas firing leaves a deficit of 772.9 kW. The pinch analysis shows that the limit is the temperature level: only 977.5 kW of the flue heat lies above 620 °C, against 1480.0 kW of reaction heat at 600 °C (Section 18).
4. **Temperature and pressure matter little above 550 °C; recovery matters a lot.** Between 550 and 800 °C the specific heat demand stays between 9.10 and 9.21 kWh/kg H~2~, because unconverted NH~3~ returns as burner heat. A PSA recovery between 0.70 and 0.95 moves the specific heat demand from 11.07 to 8.16 kWh/kg H~2~ (Sections 17 and 20.5).
5. **Ammonia sets the cost.** On the unified basis the LCOH is 4.015 USD/kg H~2~ (5.174 SGD/kg) with the supplementary fuel counted at 100 % of its lower heating value (optimistic; 4.038 and 4.063 USD/kg at 0.9225 and 0.85) and a PSA recovery of 0.85 (above the only published front for this composition; {{VAL:reference_scenario/baseline.json#psa_recovery_sensitivity/cases/1/lcoh_USD_per_kg|.3f}} and {{VAL:reference_scenario/baseline.json#psa_recovery_sensitivity/cases/2/lcoh_USD_per_kg|.3f}} USD/kg at its recoveries of 0.8192 and 0.7744), of which the NH~3~ feed is 76 %; the NH~3~ price explains about 90 % of the LCOH variance (total-effect index 0.896). Grey ammonia at 450 USD/t is not cheap enough to meet the 3.06 USD/kg NE Asia SMR marker; the break-even is 320 USD/t (277 to 379), and the sourced prices lie on both sides of it. The LCOH is below the 6.0 USD/kg delivered low-carbon benchmark, for which the break-even is 720 USD/t (Sections 32, 34 and 37). The legacy core result of 3.919 USD/kg (Section 19) is superseded for research outputs.
6. **The baseline product is not fuel-cell grade.** Its purity of 99.961 mol% follows from assumed split fractions and is below the ISO 14687:2019 grade D limits; NH~3~ polishing is required and not modelled (Section 11).
7. **The validation is useful because it can fail.** The core matrix has 137 items: 105 pass, 22 fail and 10 are informational. The {{VAL:validation/summary.json#totals/total}} Phase 2 research items ({{VAL:validation/summary.json#totals/pass}} pass, {{VAL:validation/summary.json#totals/fail}} fail, {{VAL:validation/summary.json#totals/informational}} informational, {{VAL:validation/summary.json#totals/not_available}} not available) are sorted by evidence kind (Section 38); their failures are kept and their causes quantified. AmCraX has no independent experimental validation.

Taken together, AmCraX is a transparent, re-runnable screening model of an ammonia cracker with a record of what is checked and what is assumed. It is suited to comparing design options and to teaching, not to sizing equipment or predicting plant performance (Section 41).

## 42.2 Conclusions on the four hypotheses

The answers below are read from the evidence matrix at build time (Section 40): each hypothesis opens with its plain answer and its limitations, and the formal classification follows. Plain answers state what the model computes and under which assumptions; the formal classification applies a stricter rule fixed before the analysis, under which a result that rests on an untested assumption is not counted as supported; both are published. The formal classification follows verdict methodology v2, whose roles, components, aggregation rule and general row standard were fixed before the Phase 2 analysis; the row-by-row coded rules were written afterwards and tested by an independent adversarial review, which moved three row verdicts to INCONCLUSIVE (Section 31.4). The verdicts of the preserved release (rule v1) are shown in Section 40. Table 42.1 answers the claims inside the hypotheses where the evidence allows, with the formal classification in its last column; the numbered conclusions after it state the answers, their limitations and the formal classification.

{{PLAIN_ANSWERS:ALL|42.1}}

1. **H1 End-use suitability.** {{HEADLINE:H1}} The gas turbine is not modelled: both fuels are assumed to run at the same 49.64 % efficiency, and the actual advantage depends on fuel-specific turbine performance (5 to 15 % on the earlier cost basis). Cracking with PSA would need a turbine about 12.6 percentage points more efficient (9.7 to 18.1 over 11 bases), above the only sourced value of 7.3, a single upper-bound source that is not like for like; fired cracking without PSA would need about 5.2 points, for which no source exists. Both thresholds count the cracker heat at 100 % of the fuel's lower heating value; at 0.85 they rise to about 13.4 and 6.5 points. Against the essay, exhaust-heated full cracking is about 1.3 % cheaper than direct use on the reference basis, which is not robust; whether cracking is justified where hydrogen is the product is not established (Section 33). Formal classification (rule fixed before the analysis): {{VERDICT:H1}}; it differs from the plain answer because the turbine efficiency is assumed, not computed, so the rule cannot count the result as supported.
2. **H2 Feedstock.** {{HEADLINE:H2}} The ammonia price dominates the cost (row H2-a SUPPORTED). Competitiveness with the single NE Asia SMR marker needs ammonia at or below about 320 USD/t (277 to 379), which only the low ends of the sourced price ranges reach, and against a 6.0 USD/kg delivered benchmark the break-even is 720 USD/t, so the break-even row H2-b is INCONCLUSIVE and component H2-E PARTIALLY SUPPORTED. Low-carbon ammonia is necessary for low-carbon hydrogen (H2-C SUPPORTED). Under the actual Singapore carbon tax, low-carbon ammonia earns no cost advantage, because the tax is zero for every route; under a hypothetical life-cycle price (not Singapore law) blue overtakes grey at about 33 SGD/t (range 12 to 605) and green at current prices needs about 305 SGD/t (Section 34). Formal classification (rule fixed before the analysis): {{VERDICT:H2}}.
3. **H3 Catalyst and heat integration.** {{HEADLINE:H3}} A low-temperature catalyst saves at most 25 kW of fired duty at equal conversion with heat integration (4.9 % of fired duty, 1.1 % of total external heating; Python only), resolved only for the two ruthenium catalysts, not the 17 % reported in Phase 1. Heat integration cuts the heat deficit by 44 % (total external heating by 21 %); its net saving after the exchanger cost rests on an unsourced heat-transfer coefficient, so H3-H is INCONCLUSIVE. Against the essay, with heat integration and the Ni reference at its modelled 594 °C (extrapolated law), three of the four low-temperature catalysts are dearer beyond the model resolution at derived prices and 2.5Ni0.5Ru/CeO~2~ is within resolution; against the sourced Ni design temperatures every low-temperature design is cheaper, so H3-K is INCONCLUSIVE and leans against the hypothesis at real ruthenium prices (Section 35). Formal classification (rule fixed before the analysis): {{VERDICT:H3}}.
4. **H4 Purification.** {{HEADLINE:H4}} Published PSA relations agree that higher purity costs recovery, but AmCraX computes no PSA purity-recovery relation and the purity-only cost increment changes sign within the published model's scatter. In the AmCraX flowsheet unrecovered hydrogen is burned in the cracker furnace; it covers the furnace demand below a PSA recovery of 0.765, and sourced recoveries lie on both sides of that value, so row H4-e is INCONCLUSIVE. PSA against a Pd-Ag membrane is not robust either way: it turns on the PSA recovery, with parity at {{VAL:purification/phase2_comparison.json#specs/fuel_cell_grade_D/thresholds/psa_recovery_at_parity_with_central_membrane|.3f}} (grade D) and {{VAL:purification/phase2_comparison.json#specs/industrial_99.9/thresholds/psa_recovery_at_parity_with_central_membrane|.3f}} (industrial), and PSA is far more mature (TRL 9 against 4 to 5) (Section 36). Formal classification (rule fixed before the analysis): {{VERDICT:H4}}.

## 42.3 What the evidence does not support

Under the formal rule no hypothesis is classified SUPPORTED as a whole; H3 is PARTIALLY SUPPORTED on one component only (H3-T). The two findings against the essay (exhaust-heated cracking slightly cheaper than direct use; three of four low-temperature catalysts dearer beyond resolution at derived ruthenium prices with the Ni reference at its modelled 594 °C) are computed results within stated envelopes. Several quantitative claims carried from the literature into the essay are not reproduced on AmCraX boundaries: the 40 % cost advantage of direct ammonia power (largest computed gap in the cost of electricity 16.6 %; the paper's own 39 to 44 % is a fuel-cost comparison at the turbine inlet, on which AmCraX gives 20 to 33 %, the same direction with a smaller gap), the statement that a ruthenium cracker needs no supplementary fuel while a nickel cracker does (true only for PSA recoveries of 0.756 to 0.781), and the 7.0 % lower reconversion cost of ruthenium reported by Cho et al. (2026) (Section 40.7). Future work is listed in Section 41.9.
