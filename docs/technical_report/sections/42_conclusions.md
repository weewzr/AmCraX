# 42 Conclusions

## 42.1 Conclusions on the AmCraX model

1. **A reproducible Aspen model.** The Aspen Plus V15 flowsheet is generated from a typed case object and solved without manual GUI work. All 279 core Aspen runs converged, every stored case names the input file that reproduces it, and the COM-driven temperature sweep agrees with Aspen's own Sensitivity block to 4.8 × 10^-10^ (Sections 6, 12 and 17).
2. **The baseline is heat-limited, not conversion-limited.** At 600 °C and 20 bar the equilibrium conversion is 0.9808 and the plant delivers 251.6 kg/h of H~2~. The cracker duty of 1480.0 kW dominates the 2293.1 kW of external heating. The feed/effluent exchanger recovers 612.2 kW and lowers the LCOH by 0.214 USD/kg before its own cost; after the exchanger cost the net benefit is +0.067 to +0.095 USD/kg (Sections 5, 10 and 35).
3. **Tail gas alone cannot heat the cracker.** On a first-law basis tail-gas firing leaves a deficit of 772.9 kW. The pinch analysis shows that the limit is the temperature level: only 977.5 kW of the flue heat lies above 620 °C, against 1480.0 kW of reaction heat at 600 °C (Section 18).
4. **Temperature and pressure matter little above 550 °C; recovery matters a lot.** Between 550 and 800 °C the specific heat demand stays between 9.10 and 9.21 kWh/kg H~2~, because unconverted NH~3~ returns as burner heat. A PSA recovery between 0.70 and 0.95 moves the specific heat demand from 11.07 to 8.16 kWh/kg H~2~ (Sections 17 and 20.5).
5. **Ammonia sets the cost.** On the unified basis the LCOH is 4.015 USD/kg H~2~ (5.174 SGD/kg), of which the NH~3~ feed is 76 %; the NH~3~ price explains about 90 % of the LCOH variance (total-effect index 0.896). Grey ammonia at 450 USD/t is not cheap enough to meet the 3.06 USD/kg NE Asia SMR marker; the break-even is 320 USD/t (Sections 32, 34 and 37). The legacy core result of 3.919 USD/kg (Section 19) is superseded for research outputs.
6. **The baseline product is not fuel-cell grade.** Its purity of 99.961 mol% follows from assumed split fractions and is below the ISO 14687:2019 grade D limits; NH~3~ polishing is required and not modelled (Section 11).
7. **The validation is useful because it can fail.** The core matrix has 137 items: 105 pass, 22 fail and 10 are informational. The Phase 2 research checks are sorted by evidence kind (Section 38); their failures are kept and their causes quantified. AmCraX has no independent experimental validation.

Taken together, AmCraX is a transparent, re-runnable screening model of an ammonia cracker with a record of what is checked and what is assumed. It is suited to comparing design options and to teaching, not to sizing equipment or predicting plant performance (Section 41).

## 42.2 Conclusions on the four hypotheses

The verdicts below are read from the evidence matrix at build time (Section 40). They follow verdict methodology v2, which was fixed before the Phase 2 analysis; the verdicts of the preserved release (rule v1) are shown in Section 40.

1. **H1 End-use suitability:** {{VERDICT:H1}}. Under the modelled operating conditions, direct ammonia combustion produces electricity at a lower cost than the standalone cracking pathways, with a calculated cost advantage of approximately 8 to 17 % (5 to 15 % on the earlier cost basis). This comparison assumes that the hydrogen-rich turbine operates at the same 49.64 % efficiency as the direct-ammonia turbine. The actual economic advantage depends on fuel-specific turbine performance. Cracking with PSA would need a turbine about 12.6 percentage points more efficient (9.7 to 18.1 over 11 bases), above the only sourced upper bound of 7.3; fired cracking without PSA would need about 5.2 points, for which no source exists. Against the essay, exhaust-heated full cracking is about 1.3 % cheaper than direct use on the reference basis (Section 33).
2. **H2 Feedstock:** {{VERDICT:H2}}. The ammonia price dominates the cost and competitiveness requires ammonia at or below about 320 USD/t; low-carbon ammonia is necessary for low-carbon hydrogen. Under the actual Singapore carbon tax, low-carbon ammonia earns no cost advantage, because the tax is zero for every route; under a hypothetical life-cycle price (not Singapore law) blue overtakes grey at about 33 SGD/t (range 12 to 605) and green at current prices needs about 305 SGD/t (Section 34).
3. **H3 Catalyst and heat integration:** {{VERDICT:H3}}. Heat integration cuts the heat deficit by 44 % and still pays after the exchanger cost. A low-temperature catalyst saves about 5 % of fired duty at equal conversion with heat integration, not the 17 % reported in Phase 1. Against the essay, at ruthenium prices derived from metal content the low-temperature catalysts do not pay at equal conversion; the outcome depends on the nickel reference temperature, which rests on an extrapolated rate law (Section 35).
4. **H4 Purification:** {{VERDICT:H4}}. Recovery, energy and cost are coupled through the tail gas, with a threshold recovery of 0.765. PSA against a Pd-Ag membrane is not robust either way: it turns on the PSA recovery, with parity near 0.80, and PSA is far more mature (TRL 9 against 4 to 5) (Section 36).

## 42.3 What the evidence does not support

No hypothesis is SUPPORTED as a whole. The two findings against the essay (exhaust-heated cracking slightly cheaper than direct use; low-temperature catalysts not paying at derived ruthenium prices) are computed results within stated envelopes. Several quantitative claims carried from the literature into the essay are not reproduced on AmCraX boundaries: the 40 % cost advantage of direct ammonia power (largest computed gap 16.6 %), the statement that a ruthenium cracker needs no supplementary fuel while a nickel cracker does (true only for PSA recoveries of 0.756 to 0.781), and the 7.0 % lower reconversion cost of ruthenium reported by Cho et al. (2026) (Section 40.7). Future work is listed in Section 41.9.
