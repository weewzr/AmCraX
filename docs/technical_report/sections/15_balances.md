## 15.2 Overall balances

The plant boundary is {FEED, AIR} → {H2PROD, STACK}; FEHE is internal. The overall balances, written out with the Aspen values in kg/h, kmol/h and kW, are:

* **Mass:** 1703.25 + 1966.72 = 3669.98 kg/h in; 252.96 + 3417.02 = 3669.98 kg/h out.
* **Nitrogen atoms:** 99.800 (NH~3~) + 2 × 53.854 (N~2~ in air) = 207.508 kmol/h in; 2 × 0.0489 + 2 × 103.705 = 207.508 kmol/h out.
* **Hydrogen atoms:** 3 × 99.800 + 2 × 0.200 (H~2~O) = 299.800 kmol/h in; 2 × 124.803 + 2 × 25.097 = 299.800 kmol/h out.
* **Oxygen atoms:** 0.200 + 2 × 14.316 = 28.831 kmol/h in; 25.097 + 2 × 1.867 = 28.831 kmol/h out.
* **Energy:** the boundary enthalpies plus all external duties and the pump power equal the outlet enthalpies:

$$ \underbrace{-2004.37 - 0.20}_{H_\mathrm{FEED} + H_\mathrm{AIR}} + \underbrace{739.46 + 73.66 + 1480.00 - 304.95 + 0.73 + 0 - 1520.25}_{\sum Q\ (\mathrm{VAPORIZR \ldots FLUEHX})} + \underbrace{1.85}_{W_\mathrm{PUMP}} = \underbrace{15.43 - 1549.51}_{H_\mathrm{H2PROD} + H_\mathrm{STACK}} = -1534.08\ \mathrm{kW} \qquad (15.1) $$

where $H$ is an enthalpy flow, $Q$ a block duty and $W$ the pump power (all kW). The relative closures are -5.4 × 10^-10^ (mass), 2.5 × 10^-9^ (N), -2.0 × 10^-9^ (H), -3.5 × 10^-9^ (O) and 3.2 × 10^-9^ (energy, normalised by the sum of absolute external duties). Block by block, outlet minus inlet enthalpy reproduces every reported duty, for example 902.81 - (-577.19) = 1480.00 kW for CRACKER, and the FEHE cold side gains the 612.21 kW that the hot side loses.

## 15.3 Unit checks

* **Cracker stoichiometry.** The extent is $\xi$ = 99.800 - 1.915 = 97.885 kmol/h. Eq. (13.1) predicts 0.5$\xi$ = 48.942 kmol/h N~2~ and 1.5$\xi$ = 146.827 kmol/h H~2~; S5 contains 48.942 and 146.827 kmol/h. Water passes unchanged (0.200 kmol/h).
* **PSA split.** H2PROD/S7 is 124.803/146.827 = 0.8500 for H~2~ and 0.0489/48.942 = 0.0010 for N~2~, the specified fractions (Section 11).
* **Burner air.** The tail gas needs 0.5 × 22.024 + 0.75 × 1.915 = 12.448 kmol/h O~2~; Eq. (6.1) gives 1.15 × 12.448 / 0.21 = 68.170 kmol/h air, as in AIR. The flue gas holds 14.316 - 12.448 = 1.867 kmol/h O~2~, 0.200 + 22.024 + 1.5 × 1.915 = 25.097 kmol/h H~2~O and 48.893 + 53.854 + 0.5 × 1.915 = 103.705 kmol/h N~2~, matching FLUE.

## 15.4 What the closures do and do not show

Closures of about 10^-9^ demonstrate **extraction and bookkeeping consistency**: every stream and duty was read from the right node, converted to the right unit, given the right sign and entered once. They would fail at once if a stream were mis-mapped or a duty sign reversed. They do not test the physics, because Aspen closes its own balances by construction; all 20 stored Aspen cases close to 3.9 × 10^-9^ or better (matrix items BAL-ASPEN, Section 16).

The independent physics evidence comes from recalculating Aspen results with other tools (Table 15.4). The adiabatic complete-combustion flame temperature of the Aspen tail gas and air, recalculated with Cantera and GRI-Mech 3.0 data (Goodwin et al., 2018; Smith et al., n.d.), agrees within 0.58 K over 20 cases. The flue-gas cooler duty agrees to 3.5 × 10^-4^. The pump power from a hand calculation, $W = n\,v_L\,\Delta P/\eta$ with the liquid NH~3~ volume of the CoolProp reference equation of state (Bell et al., 2014), is 0.29 % above Aspen. The exchanger UA equals Q/LMTD from the four terminal temperatures; this last item checks the consistency of the shortcut output, not heat transfer. Together with the equilibrium comparison (Section 13) and the reactor-duty reconciliation (Section 9), these are the checks that test the Aspen physics.
