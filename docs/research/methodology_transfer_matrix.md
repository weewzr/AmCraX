# Methodology transfer from nuclear engineering to AmCraX

This page assesses published computational methods from the nuclear-engineering literature for use in AmCraX: uncertainty quantification, inverse UQ and design for cost (publications of K. Shirvan and co-authors), and high-temperature heat, heat storage and integrated energy systems (publications of C. W. Forsberg and co-authors). Each row states what was read, what could transfer, whether the repository holds the data, and where no defensible transfer exists. The assessment is a literature comparison (evidence kind 3); nothing on this page is experimental validation.

## Transfers worth implementing (ranked)

1. **T1. Variance-based global sensitivity analysis of the unified techno-economic model** (from S4, with S5 grouping and S7 distribution-shape case). Sobol first-order and total indices replace the one-at-a-time tornado bars, show interactions, and group inputs into cost drivers (feed, capital, heat, catalyst, operation). All input ranges are already sourced. Feasibility high.
2. **T2a. Kinetic parameter-uncertainty propagation** (from S1). The published confidence intervals of the Lucentini et al. (2021) rate law are propagated jointly to the temperature needed for a target conversion, the catalyst mass and LCOH. Calibration is not possible with the repository data (3 points per catalyst, 4 parameters, points already used in the published fit), so this is propagation of reported uncertainty, labelled as such. Feasibility high.
3. **T2b. Bayesian inverse UQ of the Takahashi and Fujitani (2016) rate-determining-step parameters** (from S1 and S2) against the digitised figure points, with a held-out validation series. Because the catalyst mass of those experiments is not reported, only temperature dependence (activation energy) is identifiable; the result is a model-form diagnostic and is not propagated to plant cost. Feasibility medium.

## Rejected

- **S3**: Surrogate for an expensive likelihood is unnecessary: the Python PFR is cheap; Aspen calibration is out of scope.
- **S6**: Heat price range is in the abstract, but delivery temperature and cost year were not read; reactor design does not transfer.
- **S8**: Combinatorial RL optimisation has no counterpart in the low-dimensional continuous AmCraX design space.
- **F1**: FHR neutronics, fuel and fluoride-salt chemistry: no defensible transfer.
- **F2**: FIRES cost and efficiency not obtained; Singapore lacks the surplus-renewable price premise; no number to use.
- **F3**: Full text not obtainable (HTTP 403); abstract has no usable number.
- **F4**: Biorefinery system design does not touch the cracker model.
- **F5**: Nuclear hydrogen production reactor concept; no defensible transfer.
- **Lucentini calibration**: 3 points per catalyst for 4 parameters, and the points are part of the published fit: Bayesian calibration would be under-determined and double count the data; replaced by T2a propagation.

## Data the repository holds for kinetic calibration

- Lucentini et al. (2021) Table 1: 6 catalysts x 3 temperatures (400, 450, 500 degC); 2 catalysts have a published rate law (5Ni1Ru: 36.5, 86.1, 99.6 %; 2.5Ni0.5Ru: 49.7, 88.7, 99.2 %); 1 atm, 0.1 g, F/W 15.0 L g^-1 h^-1, Ar:NH3 1.2:1, 25 mL/min (reference state not stated); MS analysis +/-5 % stated (absolute or relative not stated); no replicate scatter; part of the fitting data.
- Lucentini et al. (2021) Table 3: fitted k0, Ek, K1, K4 with +/- confidence intervals for both catalysts; no covariance.
- Takahashi and Fujitani (2016) figures: Ni 33 (Fig. 3a 4, 3b 4, 3c 15, 5a 5, 1a 5); Ru 32 (Fig. 4a 3, 4b 4, 4c 15, 5b 5, 2a 5); Fig. 3a/4a 15000 h^-1 points repeat points of Figs. 3c/4c; 1 atm, SV 2000-30000 h^-1, catalyst mass and SV volume basis not reported; figure reading about +/-2 percentage points; experimental scatter not reported.
- Pressure: none with a published rate law and full specification; every 20 bar kinetic result remains an extrapolation.
